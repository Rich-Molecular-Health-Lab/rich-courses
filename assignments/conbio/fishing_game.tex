\subsection{The Ultimate Goal}\label{the-ultimate-goal}

Maximize your fishing yields by the end of the game.

\subsection{Setup}\label{setup}

\subsubsection{Groups}\label{groups}

\begin{itemize}
\tightlist
\item
  Form groups of 4-6 students each for the game.
\item
  Each group will receive:

  \begin{itemize}
  \tightlist
  \item
    1 bowl (your \emph{reservoir})
  \item
    30 fish placed in that reservoir
  \end{itemize}
\item
  Each person will recieve:

  \begin{itemize}
  \tightlist
  \item
    1 cup (your \emph{boat})
  \end{itemize}
\end{itemize}

\subsubsection{Important Biological
Rule}\label{important-biological-rule}

\begin{itemize}
\tightlist
\item
  After each fishing round in this game, any fish left in the reservoir
  double in number, but only up to a maximum of 30 fish (\emph{carrying
  capacity}).
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Round 1: Open Access
Fishing}\label{round-1-open-access-fishing}

\subsubsection{Scenario}\label{scenario}

\begin{itemize}
\tightlist
\item
  Each of you represents a fisher in a shared fishery aiming to catch as
  many fish as possible.
\item
  You will have no means of communication or coordination with other
  fishers in your fishery.
\item
  After this fishing season (the end of the round), the fish will enter
  a reproductive phase, and any that are not harvested will produce one
  offspring each.
\end{itemize}

\subsubsection{Instructions}\label{instructions}

\begin{enumerate}
\def\labelenumi{\arabic{enumi}.}
\tightlist
\item
  Privately record how many fish you will catch this round (up to 6
  allowed).
\end{enumerate}

\begin{itemize}
\tightlist
\item
  \textbf{\emph{Remember, no communicating ahead of time during this
  round!}}
\end{itemize}

\begin{enumerate}
\def\labelenumi{\arabic{enumi}.}
\setcounter{enumi}{1}
\tightlist
\item
  Simultaneously reveal everyone's card with their number.
\item
  Distribute each person's catch into their boat.
\end{enumerate}

\begin{itemize}
\tightlist
\item
  \textbf{\emph{If the cumulative group catch exceeds the number of fish
  available, distribute the fish until the reservoir is empty
  (first-come, first-served)}}
\end{itemize}

\begin{enumerate}
\def\labelenumi{\arabic{enumi}.}
\setcounter{enumi}{3}
\tightlist
\item
  Give the fish a reproductive season.
\end{enumerate}

\begin{enumerate}
\def\labelenumi{\alph{enumi}.}
\tightlist
\item
  Count the remaining fish.
\item
  Add new fish so that the remaining number has doubled in size.
\item
  Record these data (\emph{fish caught, fish remaining})
\end{enumerate}

\begin{quote}
Pause and record a prediction: \emph{How long could the fishery sustain
with this type of open-access fishing?} Write your response (1-2
sentences) on the back of the notecard with your name and today's date.
\end{quote}

\subsubsection{Context and Theory}\label{context-and-theory}

Did you notice how quickly the shared resource (your fishery) can become
depleted when everyone acts in their own self-interest? This situation
is an example of a social dilemma---a scenario where individuals benefit
most by acting selfishly, but if everyone does this, the group as a
whole suffers (\emph{The Prisoner's Dilemma}).

This dynamic is often called the Tragedy of the Commons---a term used in
conservation biology to describe what happens when shared resources
(like fisheries, forests, or wildlife populations) are overused and
degraded because no one has a personal incentive to conserve them.

\subsubsection{Modifying our Approach}\label{modifying-our-approach}

Now we will adjust our rules so that you can act more strategically to
balance your short-term choices with the population's sustainability in
mind. Pause for a moment and try to predict the \textbf{maximum number
of fish} each member of your group could afford to harvest in one season
without reducing the overall population in the long term. This concept
is known as the \textbf{\emph{Maximum Sustainable Yield (MSY)}}, and it
relies on the assumption that your fish will maintain a consistent
reproductive rate from one season to the next. In our case, the
population doubles each round, and so the MSY is reached when enough
fish are left behind to fully replenish the stock.

In the next round we will use a strategy called \textbf{Fixed Quota
Harvesting} to improve the sustainability of our yield. This is a
management strategy where the \textbf{total amount harvested is limited}
to a set number (e.g., 15 fish per round). The quota must be shared
among users, and exceeding it can lead to collapse or penalties.

\subsection{Round 2: Fixed-Quota
Harvesting}\label{round-2-fixed-quota-harvesting}

\subsubsection{Scenario}\label{scenario-1}

\begin{itemize}
\tightlist
\item
  You may now work together to set a \textbf{total fixed quota} (i.e.,
  one total for the group) to sustainably harvest each round
  (recommended quota around the MSY, e.g., 15 fish).
\item
  You still may not discuss how many fish each individual plans to catch
  to contribute to this quota, and your goal is still to maximize your
  long-term yields as an individual.
\end{itemize}

\subsubsection{Instructions}\label{instructions-1}

\begin{enumerate}
\def\labelenumi{\arabic{enumi}.}
\tightlist
\item
  Discuss openly for 1--2 minutes and agree on a fixed quota (e.g.,
  ``Our total catch limit is exactly 15 fish per round.'').
\item
  Privately record how many fish you will catch this round (up to 6
  allowed).
\end{enumerate}

\begin{itemize}
\tightlist
\item
  \textbf{\emph{Remember, still no communicating this part}}
\end{itemize}

\begin{enumerate}
\def\labelenumi{\arabic{enumi}.}
\setcounter{enumi}{2}
\tightlist
\item
  Simultaneously reveal everyone's card with their number.
\item
  This time, if the \textbf{total catch exceeds the quota}, the fishery
  immediately ``collapses'', and no fish can reproduce.
\end{enumerate}

\begin{itemize}
\tightlist
\item
  If your group's total catch is equal or less than quota, you
  distribute each person's catch again, and reproduction proceeds
  normally (fish left double, up to 30).
\item
  Record these results (\emph{fish caught, fish remaining})
\end{itemize}

\begin{enumerate}
\def\labelenumi{\arabic{enumi}.}
\setcounter{enumi}{4}
\tightlist
\item
  Pause and record a reflection: \emph{Was it challenging or easy to
  respect quotas?}
\end{enumerate}

\subsubsection{Context and Theory}\label{context-and-theory-1}

How else could you manage your group's collective harvesting to maximize
your long-term yields? One option is known as \textbf{\emph{Fixed Effort
Harvesting}}. In this approach, the game manager specifies the level of
effort (for example the number of fishers/hunters or length of a
fishing/hunting season), rather than the number of animals to be
harvested. This strategy relies on the assumption that harvest rate
increases with the amount of effort, so for our purposes, we can simply
target the quota of fish per person.

\subsection{Round 3: Fixed-Effort
Harvesting}\label{round-3-fixed-effort-harvesting}

\subsubsection{Scenario}\label{scenario-2}

\begin{itemize}
\tightlist
\item
  You may now work together to set a \textbf{fixed effort quota} (i.e.,
  a per-person fishing limit) to sustainably harvest each round
  (recommended quota around the MSY, e.g., 2 fish).
\end{itemize}

\subsubsection{Instructions}\label{instructions-2}

\begin{enumerate}
\def\labelenumi{\arabic{enumi}.}
\tightlist
\item
  No need for any secrecy this time. Instead, you need to collaborate as
  a group to collectively decide on how many fish to distribute to each
  person and then pass them out.
\item
  Give the remaining fish a reproductive season.
\end{enumerate}

\begin{enumerate}
\def\labelenumi{\alph{enumi}.}
\tightlist
\item
  Count the remaining fish.
\item
  Add new fish so that the remaining number has doubled in size.
\item
  Record these data (\emph{fish caught, fish remaining})
\end{enumerate}

\begin{enumerate}
\def\labelenumi{\arabic{enumi}.}
\setcounter{enumi}{2}
\tightlist
\item
  Pause and record a reflection: \emph{Did fixed-effort make
  sustainability easier or harder to maintain?}
\end{enumerate}
